# Outreach — finding leads, emails, calls, cadence

Sources are named so the founder can check them. Claims marked ⚠️ are secondary or directional; 🚩 marks a real conflict in the evidence. Never present an unmarked number that isn't here.

---

## 1. Where leads come from, in order

**Warm network mining first.** One sitting, target 50 names: LinkedIn connections, past colleagues, ex-clients, alumni, community Slacks, anyone who has ever asked what the founder is building. Boring, and it is the whole engine at the start.

**Then referral chains.** The referral ask is mandatory in every call debrief, in Fitzpatrick's wording: *"Who else should I talk to?"* An intro is **reputation currency** — the second rung of the commitment ladder — so an intro offered unprompted is a stronger buying signal than enthusiasm.

**Cold sourcing last, and only when warm is genuinely exhausted.** The evidence says more names is not the constraint:

- Reply rate by list size: **<50 contacts 5.8%** · 50–200 ~4–5% · 200–500 ~3% · **500–1,000+ 2.1%** (Woodpecker, 20M+ emails ⚠️). Corroborated independently by Belkins' campaign-size data. **Small hand-built lists beat big ones by roughly 3x.**
- Warm introductions convert **2–3x better than cold email** (Aaron Epstein, YC Startup School ⚠️).
- *"better to send 100 targeted emails... rather than a thousand untargeted emails."* — Epstein, YC.

**Best-responding segments** (Belkins, 7,530,489 emails, 2025): Founders/Owners **0.57%** vs C-level 0.42% vs VPs 0.32%. Company size 0–10 employees **0.72%** · 11–50 **0.49%** · 10,000+ **0.22%**. Small companies and founders reply most — which is usually exactly where an early-stage ICP sits.

## 2. The first email

**Structure: Personalization → Problem → Solution → CTA.** (Gong × 30 Minutes to President's Club, 85,000,000 cold emails, 2025.)

| Element | Rule | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | **Under 4 words**, lowercase, phrased like an internal email — "trial delays", "agenda drift", "2026 forecasts" | 85M emails |
| Length | **Under 100 words**; *"You shouldn't have to scroll to read it on your phone"* | 85M emails |
| Sentences | **3–4 is the money zone** | 85M emails |
| Pronouns | "You" beats "we" — *"'We' language leads to pitching and that kills your cold emails"* | 85M emails |
| Language | Problem language over buzzwords | On calls: 16% vs 5.5% success |
| CTA | Ask for **interest or an offer**, not a meeting | Interest CTA **30%** vs specific-date meeting **15%** vs open-ended **13%** |
| ROI framing | Don't | −15% |
| Format | Plain text. No images, no logos, no template chrome | Kazanjy; Kalungi |

🚩 **Length is the most contradicted variable in this literature.** Lavender says 25–50 words; the 85M-email study says <100 / 3–4 sentences; Gong's own older guide said ~150; Kazanjy's shipped templates run 170–290. The newer and larger dataset supersedes the older one and the direction of travel across every dataset is *shorter*. **Target 50–100 words for touch 1, with Kazanjy's tone rather than his length.**

**Empty subject lines** get more opens but **12% lower replies** — *"This is clickbait."*

**Personalisation type by seniority** ⚠️ (Gong, 30,000+ emails): individual-based personalisation more than doubles reply rate for ICs and managers but adds only ~50% for director+; **company-based personalisation triples reply rates with director+**; industry-based adds 88%.

**C-level are 30.2% less likely to reply than non-executives** ⚠️. Execs spend *"less than three seconds"* deciding to open and *"no more than nine seconds"* reading.

### Tone — Kazanjy's rule, verbatim

> "The best templates do all of this in a plainspoken, dare I say fun, way that speaks to the prospect candidly, authoritatively, and as a peer. They avoid bullshit jargon-speak and unnecessarily 'businessy' communication patterns."

> "These emails should have the tone and candor of a message from one CEO to another (even if it's actually from a founder to the 'CEO' of a function within the target organization)."

### The warm-intro protocol, verbatim (Kazanjy)

> "Don't ask the introducer to email introduce you directly, as you want to other side to opt in to engaging rather than being dropped blindly into their lap. And don't rely on the introducer to execute the outreach on their own, since she doesn't know much about your pitch and argument. You simply want her to be in charge of passing your message along, with commentary about why you're a great guy."

Write the forwardable paragraph *for* the introducer, and include *"some sort of specialness, like maybe it's a closed beta and no one else knows about it, and you prospected the target especially."*

## 3. Cadence

**~6 touches over 14–28 days, then stop.**

> "Never send one email and stop. Send ~6 emails across 14-28 days to maximize cold email replies (but after that, reply rates drop below 0.5% so give it a rest)." — Gong × 30MPC, 85M emails

Every credible source converges on **5–7 touches inside 2–4 weeks**: Yesware (10M threads ⚠️) days 3/7/11/15/19/22; Woodpecker (20M+ ⚠️) 4–7 touchpoints; Gong (300M calls) says stop dialling after 5.

**Kazanjy's founder-shaped version**, verbatim — the most aggressive and the right size for one person:

> "an initial email and call on day one, followed by a call on day two, and then maybe a call and a voice mail on day three. From there, you might skip a day, and then email on day five, followed by an 'I'm breaking up with you' email on day seven."

**Most replies come after touch 1.** Kazanjy citing Outreach.io: the **second** email draws the highest response — **18% vs 12%** for the first — then 17%, 17%, 13% through the fifth. ⚠️ "70% of cold email replies come AFTER the first email" (30MPC/Gong). Woodpecker: the **first follow-up at 8.4%** is the highest-performing single step; no follow-ups 4.1% vs 3–5 follow-up steps 8.3%. **A one-and-done founder email throws away most of its own yield.**

### What to say on touches 3–7

> "Keep your bump emails 1 sentence long." · "Don't keep pitching value. Bump your previous email with 'thoughts,' rephrase your CTA, or eventually send a breakup in your latter emails." — 85M emails

Kazanjy's version: each follow-up carries **one new thought**, not a nag — split the narrative into separate short emails, one idea each, so *"you'll never have to send a dumb-as-nails 'Just following up on this…'"*

🚩 Gong's own data conflicts with itself here: the word "thoughts" reduces meetings booked by 20% *as a CTA*, while the 85M report recommends bumping with it. Likely reconciliation: bad as a first-email CTA, fine as a one-line bump. Unsettled — treat as a coin flip, not a rule.

### The break-up email — well supported

- *"Bump Emails with breakup language Nearly Double Your Email Reply Rate."* — 85M emails
- Kazanjy's mechanism, verbatim: *"they're used to sales reps continuing to follow up ad infinitum, so they feel that they always have the option to respond to you later. If you specifically say, 'I'm not going to email you anymore, but I have conviction that this is relevant to you and will make you more successful in what you do,' it can force a prospect to actually consider your arguments."*

⚠️ The edge may be partly **last-touch attribution** — it's the final email, so it inherits credit for interest built across all prior touches. Noted in the 30MPC material itself.

**Close.com's short version, verbatim** (their other templates are too long for a small deal):

```
Hey [Name],

I was really looking forward to talking with you about [Product] that can get you
[x benefit], but I haven't received any response. I'm sure you're busy, so this will be my
last email to you.

If you change your mind and would like to give [Product] a try, please let me know.

Always happy to chat.

Best,
[Your Name]
```

Placement: roughly 7–10 days from the first email.

### ⭐ The single highest-leverage cadence finding for a solo founder

Adding calls and voicemails to an email sequence, from Gong's 300M-call dataset:

| Mechanism | Effect |
|---|---|
| Cold calls added to an email sequence | Email reply **1.81% → 3.44%** (1.9x) |
| Leaving a voicemail | Email reply **2.73% → 5.87%** (~2.15x) |
| Cost of voicemails | Future connect rate 7.18% → 5.17% (−28%) |

> "Leave 1-2 voicemails to boost email replies, then rotate numbers, dial early, and move on after 5 dials to protect your connect rate."

**Reframe for the founder: the call is not primarily a meeting-booking channel — it is a reply-rate amplifier for the emails they were already sending.** One dial and one voicemail per prospect roughly doubles email reply rate at near-zero marginal cost. (The meeting-booking economics of cold calling alone are brutal: average rep needs **1,402 dials per meeting**, top rep **45**.)

## 4. Cold call openers

🚩 **The famous stat is stale.** The widely-quoted *"'How've you been?' = 6.6x"* comes from a 2019 study of 90,380 calls and has been superseded by Gong's 300M+ call dataset:

| Opener | Success |
|---|---|
| "Did I catch you at a bad time?" | **2.15%** — the worst |
| "How's your day going?" | 7.6% |
| Permission-based opener | 11.18% |
| "Heard the name tossed around?" | **11.24%** |

**Tailored permission opener, verbatim:**

> "Emma, I just finished reading your press release about your new office opening. I'm gonna be honest, this is a cold call, but it is a well-researched one. Can I get 30 seconds to tell you why that press release prompted me to call you specifically, then you can tell me whether or not it makes sense for us to speak?"

**Peer-context opener, verbatim, with both branches:**

> "Hey Jane, we work with a few Sequoia companies. It's Armand from 30MPC, heard the name tossed around?"
> If yes: "Oh good, we'd normally be working with someone like you by now. What'd you hear?"
> If no: "Ha! Guess I'm not as popular as I thought. Well, the reason for my call is I work with a few other Sequoia portfolio companies, and typically… [explain the problem you solve]"

### After the opener (300M calls)

| Pitch content | Success |
|---|---|
| Buzzwords ("single source of truth", "all-in-one platform", "revolutionize") | 5.5% |
| Social proof ("we helped [company] do…") | 12% |
| **Problem language** ("frustrated", "anxious", "hate", "overwhelmed") | **16%** |

> "Explain a problem in such triggering detail that it becomes impossible to ignore your solution."

### Call mechanics (Gong, 100k cold calls)

- Successful cold calls run **5:50**; unsuccessful **3:14**.
- **Talk:listen on a successful cold call is 55:45.** *"This is the one time when you should talk more than you listen."* 🚩 Do not import the 70/30 listen ratio from demo advice here — on a cold call it means dead air.
- *"There's zero statistical difference in the number of questions asked on successful and unsuccessful cold calls."*
- "We" used 35% more and "our" 55% more than "my" in successful calls.
- **Misapplied social proof drops early-stage win rates 47%** — *"Pick logos that belong to your prospect's 'tribe' – companies with similar industry, size, and geography."*
- **Risk-reversal language** ("try it for 30 days with no obligation") **+32%**.
- Meeting-booked rate rises with duration: 2 min 4% · 4 min 13% · 6 min 21% · **10 min 30%**.

### Objections at the door (300M calls)

Frequency: Not interested **17%** · Product fit **17%** · No budget **16%** · Not my responsibility **13%** · hang up **11%**. **The top five are 74% of all objections** — rehearse exactly those.

> "The median successful call duration is 4.8 minutes, which means you'll need to handle a minimum of 2 objections to land the meeting."

Gatekeepers reduce success by **39%**.

**Kazanjy's objection scripts, verbatim:**

> **"Call me later."** — "I'd be happy to do that. But if possible, maybe we could spend thirty seconds right now to make sure that this is relevant to you. And if it's not, in a quarter, I won't unnecessarily bug you, and you won't have to deal with irrelevant outreach. Great, right?"

> **"I don't have budget."** — "That's not a problem, because I really just want to understand more about your organization's way of <problem space>. If it turns out we're relevant, there will be time to talk about that later. Based on what I saw about your <signifier of demand>, I am confident that a ten-minute conversation will definitely be worth your time! Can we schedule something for Thursday?"

> **"Just send me some information."** — "I'm totally happy to do that. But if I do, I'm going to do a bang-up job, and it'll take me thirty minutes to put together something very personalized to you. I don't want to do that if our solution is not relevant. Can I take thirty seconds to explain what we do, and ask some questions to make sure this is a relevant conversation? That way if it's not, I won't be in your inbox chasing you and asking about information that wasn't even helpful."

> **"We already use <competitor>."** — "That's great! [Competitor] has historically been the benchmark for [category]. The good news is that [you] can help you [differentiated outcome]… Sounds pretty helpful, right? I'd love to get twenty minutes on the calendar to show you how [you] is like [competitor], with rocket boosters attached."

> **"How much does it cost?"** — "While the solution does have a cost, it's really contingent on a variety of factors, associated with how much value a given customer would get out of it. Typically people find it most effective to schedule a twenty-minute demo so we can get a better sense of whether the solution is relevant and how helpful it could be to your business. Do you have twenty minutes this Thursday or Friday for that conversation?"

> **"I can't make that decision."** — "Got it! Well, it sounds like this is relevant to <company>, but that you might be the wrong person to talk to about it. I tried to use LinkedIn to figure out who would be the most relevant contact, but I guess I got it wrong. Which of your colleagues should I be engaging instead?"

> **Graceful retreat** — "Okay, I understand. It sounds like this is something that might not be a priority right now. My only goal was to seek to understand your business pain … and see how we can help with a fifteen-minute scheduled call. I know that you weren't expecting my call, but are you sure you wouldn't be open to a conversation at a more appropriate time?"

**Pre-empting an objection with humour**, verbatim: *"We got to the point where we might start a call with 'I'm not calling from a recruiting agency, so let's just get that out of the way up front!'"*

### Booking the meeting, verbatim (Kazanjy)

> "Wonderful! Are you in front of your calendar? I have availability on Thursday and Friday morning, if that could work for you. It would be best for us to block thirty minutes of time. Excellent, I'll send you a meeting invite for that time with the relevant details in it. Make sure you accept it when it comes through so I know that time works and the appointment gets on your calendar."

And the anti-pattern, verbatim:

> "Don't rely on scheduling over email; this is a rookie error—being so excited (or scared) that you pull a 'Great, I'll email you some times that could work!' That turns into email hell, and you just blew that demo you set."

> "Make sure that you're not setting the appointment too far in advance; anything over a week out heavily degrades attendance rates. Usually a good time frame is a couple days out."

Gong singles out **"Do you have your calendar handy?"** as *"the most powerful question you can use to wrap a cold call."*

**Pre-call reminder that raises show rates**, verbatim:

> "Hey, Sarah. Looking forward to speaking with you and going over [Product]. I understand that when we reached out to you to schedule this call, you had expressed specific interest in learning more about [X] and [Y]. I will cover these in depth with you."

## 5. Voicemail

Always paired with an email sent at the same time — *"it may inspire your prospect to simply hit 'reply' on the email that came in at the same time."*

**The call-to-drive-email-reply voicemail, verbatim (30MPC):**

> "Hey Jason, I work with a few other partners in the [X] offices. It's Armand from [Company]. I'm going to send you an email after this thing. No need to call me back, just reply there and let me know if what I'm sending you is even moderately interesting."

**Kazanjy's, verbatim:**

```
Hey there! It's {{NAME}} from [Company] again.
I wanted to follow up on my previous message regarding [Product].
On paper, it seems like your company would be a great fit for our tool, given your
{{specific need}}, and I just want to chat for a quick minute to see if scheduling you for
a live web demo would make sense.
Once again, {{NAME}} calling from [Company], {{PHONE}}, that's {{PHONE}}.
I look forward to speaking with you soon.
```

## 6. Timing

- **Wednesday and Thursday: +49% booking rates.** Best hours for *converting* **11am–12pm and 4–5pm**; best hours for *connecting* are earlier, ~8–10am (different metrics, both true — dial early to connect, mid-day to convert).
- Weekday over weekend: **+23.3%** ⚠️.
- Peak reply month February (0.54%), trough December (0.35%) ⚠️.
- Always compute against the **buyer's** clock, and against the founder's real overlap window from `product.md`.

## 7. Calibration — what "good" looks like

| Metric | Number | Population |
|---|---|---|
| Cold emails per meeting, average rep | **344** | Gong/30MPC, 85M — SDR population |
| Cold emails per meeting, top rep | **~40** (8.1x the average rep's meeting count) | same |
| "Gold standard" reply rate | **10%+** | Gong |
| Individually written, coached 1:1 emails | **3.4% → 4.3%** (A-graded), up to 5.4% for some buyer roles | Lavender, 231,818 emails |
| Agency bulk outbound | **0.45%** | Belkins, 7.5M emails |

🚩 **These differ by ~45x and the variance is methodological, not real.** Belkins changed its own denominator (opens → total sends) between years, which accounts for most of the apparent collapse you see quoted online. The widely-cited 3.43% figure appears on three vendors' sites but traces to **one** source republished.

**A solo founder writing individually to a hand-built list is the Lavender population, not the Belkins population.** Benchmark against high-single-digit reply rates. Anyone quoting 0.45% is quoting an agency running 7.5 million sends.

And Lavender's own conclusion, which is the right one given a 45x published spread:

> "The trick to establishing benchmarks does not concern someone else's metrics."

Work backwards from the revenue target through the founder's own measured rates, and replace each imported benchmark with their own number as soon as there are 20+ observations of it.

## Do not use — commonly repeated, unverified

- *"80% of successful sales take five or more follow-ups"* and *"60% of customers reject an offer four times before buying"* — trace to the Marketing Donut / "National Sales Executive Association" lineage; never substantiated with a published dataset.
- *"44% of salespeople stop after one try"* — same lineage.
- *"'How've you been?' converts 6.6x"* — real, but superseded (see §4).
- Any LinkedIn connection-accept or InMail reply benchmark. **No credible dataset was found.** Circulating figures (~30% acceptance, 10–25% InMail response) come from vendor marketing with no disclosed methodology. Measure the founder's own rate from week one instead.
- *"Hope all is well" adds 24% to replies* 🚩 — counterintuitive, widely disputed, single-study correlation.
